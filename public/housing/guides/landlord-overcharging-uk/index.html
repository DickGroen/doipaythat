Landlord charging for repairs — what tenants can and cannot be billed for

Landlords have responsibilities for repairs that they cannot pass on to tenants. Understanding which charges are legitimate — and which are not — can save you significant money.

5 min read

What landlords are responsible for

Under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, landlords are legally responsible for maintaining the structure and exterior, heating and hot water systems, sanitation, and electrical wiring. These costs cannot be passed on to tenants.

What tenants can legitimately be charged for

  • Damage caused by the tenant beyond fair wear and tear
  • Specific items agreed in the tenancy agreement
  • Replacement of items damaged by the tenant
Fair wear and tearA landlord cannot charge for deterioration resulting from normal use over time. Only damage beyond this can be charged to a tenant.

Disputing unfair deposit deductions

If your landlord has made deductions you believe are unfair, you can dispute them through your deposit protection scheme's free dispute resolution service. Your deposit must be protected in a government-approved scheme — if it isn't, your landlord may owe you compensation of up to three times the deposit amount.
