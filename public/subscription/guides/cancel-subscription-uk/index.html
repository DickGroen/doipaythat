UK Subscriptions

Can't cancel a subscription in the UK? Here's what to do.

UK consumer law gives you the right to cancel subscriptions — and companies that make it deliberately difficult to cancel may be in breach of FCA rules and the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024.

5 min read

Your cancellation rights

If you signed up to a subscription online, by phone, or by post, you have a 14-day cooling-off right under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013. After that period, your right to cancel depends on the subscription terms — but there are several legal protections that apply regardless.

Under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, businesses must make it as easy to cancel a subscription as it was to sign up. If you signed up online with one click, you must be able to cancel online with one click.

If the company won't let you cancel

  • Send a written cancellation notice by email — this creates a record and may be sufficient regardless of the company's preferred process
  • If you pay by card, contact your bank or card provider about stopping future payments
  • If you pay by direct debit, you can cancel the direct debit with your bank directly
  • If you pay by continuous payment authority (CPA), your card provider must cancel it on request
  • Report to Trading Standards if the company is making cancellation unreasonably difficult
Continuous Payment Authority Many subscription companies use a Continuous Payment Authority — a recurring charge to your debit or credit card. Under FCA rules, your bank or card provider must cancel a CPA when you ask. You do not need the company's permission to cancel a CPA.

Charges after you've cancelled

If a company continues to charge you after you have given clear written notice of cancellation, you may be entitled to a refund of those charges. Write to the company requesting a refund and referencing your cancellation notice. If unsuccessful, a chargeback through your bank or card provider is often the most effective next step.
